# World Bible — The Aetherbound Frontier

## Premise

Centuries ago an event called the **Sundering** shattered an empire that once spanned the continent. The aether — a residue of that catastrophe — now seeps from the broken places of the world, warping geography, weather, and memory. The player is a **Frontier-born**, raised on the edge of the old empire's collapsed roads, drawn outward by an omen at birth.

The world rewards curiosity and punishes carelessness in equal measure. Every biome is a record of something that went wrong.

## The Twenty Regions

These are the 20 World Geography features (FR-0001 → FR-0020), expanded into setting context. Each is a discoverable place with a purpose, faction connection, and mystery.

1. **Ancient crater lake** — birthplace of the Sundering; ringed by Temple Oracle ruins.
2. **Floating basalt arch** — a relic the Skyship Guild uses as a navigation beacon.
3. **Glacier-carved canyon** — overland trade shortcut, contested by bandits.
4. **Sunken imperial road** — the empire's grand highway, now half-submerged.
5. **Giant-root forest maze** — home of the Forest Clan, hostile to industry.
6. **Obsidian desert ridge** — Miners' Union claim; volcanic glass economy.
7. **Mist-covered highland** — Imperial Remnant's last fortress.
8. **Tidal mangrove labyrinth** — Pirate Cove's hidden inlets.
9. **Crystal salt flat** — Nomad Caravan trade hub.
10. **Storm-split mountain pass** — Weather-locked; only crossable in calm windows.
11. **Abandoned canal network** — water-puzzle dungeon territory.
12. **Volcanic glass beach** — high-value resource node; thermal hazard.
13. **Overgrown battlefield** — ghost procession event location.
14. **Skyfall meteor field** — random rare-resource event biome.
15. **Hollow titan skeleton** — dungeon and lore site.
16. **Moonlit mushroom valley** — alchemy ingredient supply.
17. **Wind-eroded mesa** — glider thermal network.
18. **Hidden geothermal spring** — rest/morale sanctuary.
19. **River-delta trading fork** — Merchant League capital.
20. **Haunted orchard basin** — moral-choice questline anchor.

## The Ten Factions

These are the 10 Faction Politics features (FR-0361 → FR-0370), with one-line identities:

| # | Faction | Wants | Hates |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Merchant League | Open trade lanes | Tariffs, piracy |
| 2 | Forest Clan | Wilderness intact | Logging, mining |
| 3 | Skyship Guild | Airspace monopoly | Unlicensed flyers |
| 4 | Ruin Scholars | Knowledge of pre-Sundering | Looters |
| 5 | Bandit Coalition | Survival | Order |
| 6 | Imperial Remnant | Restoration | All other factions |
| 7 | Temple Oracle | Spiritual order | Aether misuse |
| 8 | Miners' Union | Fair wages | Corporate owners |
| 9 | Pirate Cove | Freedom of seas | Skyship Guild |
| 10 | Nomad Caravan | Safe migration | Border walls |

Faction reputation is tracked per-player on a -100 → +100 axis with three thresholds: hostile, neutral, allied. Reputation changes broadcast to NPC dialogue, merchant prices, checkpoint access, and ending eligibility.

## The Companion Roster

A scholar, a forest scout, a former imperial soldier, a bandit-turned-cook, a young oracle, a skyship engineer, a mute beast-tamer, a wandering bard, an exiled noble, and a clockwork construct. Each has a recruitment quest, a loyalty mission, a romance flag (which can be set to platonic), and an epilogue triggered by ending choice.

## Tone

Adventurous, melancholy, hopeful. Violence has weight; mercy has weight. Humor exists in companion banter and merchant haggling, never in suffering. The world is rebuildable but not innocent.

## Themes

- **Memory vs. progress** — the empire's ghosts vs. the frontier's freedom.
- **Cost of curiosity** — every secret door has a price.
- **Community as power** — settlements outperform lone heroes in the endgame.
